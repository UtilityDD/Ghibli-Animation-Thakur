## 📖 1. Source Material Authentication & Structure

The episodic structure is directly grounded in the primary historical text:
* **Source Document**: `param bismoy balok thakur.xlsx`
* **Author**: Professor P. K. Roy (রায়দা) — First-class Colliery Manager & Indian School of Mines scholar.
* **Biographical Sources**:
  * Direct childhood masters: Late Krishnadhan Saha, Late Prakash Chandra Bol, Late Sanatan Hazra.
  * Immediate family: Sister Smt. Gita Bhattacharya, Cousin Asit Bhattacharya, Elder Brother Late Dwijendra Chandra Chakrabarti.
  * Disciples & Observers: Ustad Bahadur Hossain Khan, Ashu Majumdar, Binoy Som, Bhupendra Nath Roy, Indra Nath Sen, Barindra Kumar Ghosh.

### Two-Part Macro Architecture
1. **Part 1 (পর্ব ১ — উজানচর-কৃষ্ণনগর পর্ব)**: Chapters 5 to 22
   * Childhood divine play (*Balya Leela*), manifestation of the 8 Siddhis, village primary school life, nature & animal harmony, and rural Bengali pastoral atmosphere in Ujanchar-Krishnanagar.
2. **Part 2 (পর্ব ২ — ঢাকা ও বিশ্বপরিক্রমা পর্ব)**: Chapters 23 to 38
   * Youth, social reforms, interactions with freedom fighters, Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose, scientists, universal healthcare, and Swamibagh.

### Episode 1: মহা আবির্ভাব ও দীপান্বিতা (The Divine Advent on Kali Puja Night)
* **Source Chapters**: Chapters 8 & 9 (`মাতুলালয় ও পৈতৃকভিটা`, `মহা আবির্ভাব`)
* **Setting**: Kaulipara / Medinimandal, ancestral Vedic household. Deep autumn night of Dipanwita Kali Puja.
* **Plot Synopsis**:
  The ancestral home is vibrant with festival preparations—dhak, dhol, and sanai resound. As midnight approaches, Tantradhar sits for the worship. Mata Charushila Devi, heavily pregnant, carries a brass plate of sugar crystals across the mud veranda. She stumbles in haste; the plate clatters to the ground. In that auspicious midnight moment, amidst the aroma of incense and sound of conch shells, Balak Thakur manifests in pristine divine radiance.
* **Atmospheric Visuals**: Warm candlelight glow, flickering earthen lamps (*pradeep*), twilight mist, vintage rural terracotta courtyards, Studio Ghibli nostalgic warmth.

---

### Episode 2: অষ্টসিদ্ধি ও লঘিমা (The Child of Eight Siddhis)
* **Source Chapters**: Chapters 10, 11 & 12 (`অষ্টসিদ্ধির প্রকাশ`, `ধ্যানমগ্ন শিশু—লঘিমা`, `তত্ত্বরূপী শিশু`)
* **Setting**: The quiet inner courtyard of Krishnanagar, pond edge, morning sunshine.
* **Plot Synopsis**:
  At age 2, the child manifests effortless bi-location: Mata Charushila puts the toddler to sleep on the cot, but stepping into the courtyard, finds him already playing happily outside. Returning him to bed, the same instant phenomenon repeats. One afternoon, the mother cannot find the toddler anywhere; panic ensues until they discover him deep in the garden, seated in motionless *Samadhi* meditation. As Binoy Som watches, the child plays innocently with turtles in the pond, communicating in the unspoken language of nature.
* **Visual Highlights**: Serene pond ripples, floating water lilies, toddler Thakur smiling at pond creatures with boundless innocent warmth.

---

### Episode 3: তিতাসের পাড়ে ও জাতি-সাপ (The Cobra Guardian & The Titas Storm)
* **Source Chapters**: Chapter 13 (`বিস্ময়কর শিশু—নিয়ম লঙ্ঘন—তিতাসের পাড়ে অদ্ভুত ঘটনা`)
* **Setting**: Bank of the river Titas (তিতাসের পাড়), vast open meadow, river crossing.
* **Plot Synopsis**:
  A 7-cubit giant cobra emerges from the banyan grove. Far from frightening the child, it follows Balak Thakur everywhere like a loyal pet dog, coiling peacefully beside him in the courtyard and escorting him to school. On a spring evening on the banks of the Titas, several hundred villagers gather around Thakur under the warm glow of hurricane lanterns (*lontrhon*). A sudden tempest arises; the storm wind swirls around Thakur and he vanishes across the river. Across the churning water, his voice calls. Unable to send a ferry boat, the devotees cry out. In an instant, Thakur is back in his seat, unharmed, holding fresh green cucumbers (*kheera*) from the distant fields for all as prasad.
* **Visual Highlights**: Dramatic storm clouds, swaying palm groves, glowing glass lanterns in the dark, serene child returning in brilliant golden backlight.

---

### Episode 4: লববাবুর পাঠশালা ও একু হাজরার স্কুল (The Village Pathshalas)
* **Source Chapters**: Chapters 14, 15 & 16 (`পাঠশালায় বিদ্যারম্ভ`, `একু হাজরার স্কুল`)
* **Setting**: Lob Babu's tin-roof schoolhouse (1926) & Eku Hazra's kinder-pathshala in Dogachhi.
* **Plot Synopsis**:
  At age 5, Pita Surendra Chandra enrolls Thakur in Lob Babu's Pathshala. While fond of the masters, Thakur cannot bear seeing fellow students harshly punished with canes. He seeks harmony and moves with his mother to maternal uncle's village Dogachhi, entering Eku Hazra's school. Here, he meets Ashu Majumdar, imparting spiritual solace and demonstrating the profound power of child innocence over dogmatic rote learning.
* **Visual Highlights**: Chalk dust, wooden slates (*patari*), bamboo writing quills, open tin-window frames looking out onto emerald paddy fields.

---

### Episode 5: সনাতন মাষ্টার ও অভিন্ন দরদ (Sanatan Master's Tears & Universal Love)
* **Source Chapters**: Chapters 17, 18, 19 & 20 (`সনাতন মাষ্টারের কান্না`, `সহস্রারের সূর্য`, `সামাদের পিতা`)
* **Setting**: Sanatan Hazra's Pathshala, village lanes of Ujanchar.
* **Plot Synopsis**:
  Sanatan Hazra, a devoted teacher, weeps openly on the final examination day because Balak Thakur has passed Class 3 and must leave his school. Overcome with compassion for his teacher's grief, Thakur willingly refuses promotion to stay another year in Class 3, comforting Sanatan: *"What does it matter, Mastermoshai? I shall remain right here with you."* When Samad's destitute father falls into agony, Thakur transcends all barriers of caste and religion, bringing life-saving relief and expounding his vision of a society free from exploitation and division.
* **Visual Highlights**: Tearful reunion on the wooden school bench, golden sunset over rural paths, universal human embrace.


### Character 1: Mata Charushila Devi (মাতা চারুশীলা দেবী)
* **Archetype**: The Divine Bengali Mother (Age 26–32).
* **Physical Proportions**: 6.2 Heads tall, graceful, maternal, gentle posture.
* **Attire**: Handwoven white cotton *taant* sari with vermilion red border (*laal-paar shari*), draped over the head (*ghomta*), red conch shell bangles (*shakha-pola*), small red round bindi.
* **Facial Features**: Soft compassionate eyes, gentle loving smile, classic Bengali oval countenance, radiant inner peace.

### Character 2: Pita Surendra Chandra Chakrabarti (পিতা সুরেন্দ্রচন্দ্র)
* **Archetype**: The Pious Sanskrit Scholar & Priest (Age 32–38).
* **Physical Proportions**: 6.5 Heads tall, lean, dignified, slightly stooped scholarly posture.
* **Attire**: Pristine white unstitched *dhoti*, bare upper body draped with white cotton *uttoriyo*, white sacred thread (*upavita*) across left shoulder.
* **Facial Features**: Modest mustache, calm observant dark eyes, sandalwood tilak on forehead, serene humility.

### Character 3: Sanatan Hazra Mastermoshai (সনাতন হাজরা)
* **Archetype**: The Reverent Rural Village Teacher (Age 45–50).
* **Physical Proportions**: 6.0 Heads tall, warm, approachable, fatherly.
* **Attire**: Faded cream cotton *kurta/panjabi*, knee-length dhoti, round steel-rimmed reading spectacles perched on nose, carrying a bamboo cane or roll of student slates.
* **Facial Features**: Kind laugh lines around eyes, deeply emotional expression, tears of devotion, grey-streaked temples.

### Character 4: The Faithful Cobra (সাত হাত জাতি-সাপ)
* **Archetype**: Sacred Nature Guardian & Gentle Companion.
* **Visual Design**: 7-cubit dark glossy olive-charcoal Indian Spectacled Cobra (*Naja naja*), warm cream underbelly, soft rounded Ghibli line art, gentle curious eyes (never menacing or demonic).
* **Rig Mechanics**: 16-bone spline IK spine chain for smooth gliding locomotion and gentle coiled rest.
